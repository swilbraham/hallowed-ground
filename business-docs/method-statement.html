Method Statement

Memorial Cleaning, Lichen Removal & Restoration

1. Scope of Works

This method statement covers the standard methods used by EverRest Memorial for non-invasive cleaning, biological growth removal, hand re-lettering and floral tribute placement on memorials in [Cemetery / Local Authority].

Stone repair, re-fixing of leaning or fallen memorials and the setting of new memorials are excluded from this statement — these works are undertaken only by a NAMM-registered partner under separate documentation.

2. Personnel & Insurance

  • All work carried out by [Name(s) of operatives], sole trader / employee of EverRest Memorial.
  • Operatives hold a current Basic DBS certificate.
  • Public Liability insurance of £5,000,000, certificate enclosed.
  • Lone-working protocol: scheduled check-ins by phone every 2 hours with a designated contact during site visits.

3. Cleaning Method (general)

  1. Stone identification. The stone type (granite, marble, limestone, sandstone, slate) is identified before any product is applied. Each stone type dictates the cleaning approach.
  2. Dry brushing. Loose dirt, dust, leaf debris and surface growth removed with a soft natural-bristle hand brush, top down.
  3. Pre-wetting. The stone is fully saturated with clean water from a low-pressure pump sprayer so that any chemical applied sits on the surface rather than penetrating the pores.
  4. Biocide application. A heritage-approved biocide (D2 Biological Solution or equivalent) applied by pump sprayer in line with the manufacturer's instructions. No diluted household cleaners, bleach, acids or alkaline products are used.
  5. Dwell. Product is left to dwell for 10–15 minutes. No mechanical action during dwell time.
  6. Gentle hand action. Soft natural-bristle brushes in light circular motions only. No wire brushes, no abrasive pads, no power tools.
  7. Rinsing. Full rinse with clean water from a low-pressure sprayer, top to bottom, multiple passes until run-off is clear.
  8. Photographic record. Before-and-after photographs taken from consistent angles and supplied to the family.

4. Lichen, Algae & Moss Removal

The above process is repeated at intervals of 2–4 weeks where deep biological growth is present. No attempt is made to scrub growth off in a single visit — biocides continue to act on lichen for several weeks after application. The family is informed of this in writing before work begins, so they understand that visible residue may remain for up to six weeks before being fully lifted.

5. Re-lettering

  • Surface prepared by dry brushing only — no abrasive abrasion of the inscription channel.
  • Hand-applied lead-free enamel paint or, where specified, 23.5ct loose gold leaf with traditional oil size.
  • Existing colour matched as closely as possible to the original inscription.
  • Letters not enlarged, deepened or modified; only the existing form is refreshed.

6. Floral Tributes

  • Existing dead floral material removed and disposed of in the cemetery's designated waste areas.
  • Fresh flowers, silk arrangements or wreaths placed without digging, anchoring or fixing into the ground.
  • No alteration to the grave plot itself.

7. Site Conduct

  • Work undertaken with quiet respect for surrounding graves and any visitors present.
  • No power tools, generators or noise-producing equipment used.
  • All materials, water buckets and tools removed at the end of each visit.
  • Adjacent graves are not walked on, leant on or used to support equipment.
  • Any visible damage, instability or concern noted during work is reported in writing to the cemetery office and to the family before any further action.

8. Products & COSHH

The only chemical product carried on site is D2 Biological Solution (or an equivalent heritage-approved biocide). Safety Data Sheet held on the operative's vehicle and available on request. No mixing of products is undertaken on site.

9. Waste & Site Reinstatement

All rinse water is allowed to soak naturally into the surrounding ground. No solid waste is left on site. Removed dead floral material is placed in the cemetery's green-waste bins. Tools are rinsed off-site.
