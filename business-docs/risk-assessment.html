Risk Assessment

Memorial Cleaning & Restoration Activities

Activity

Hand cleaning, biological growth removal, hand re-lettering and floral tribute placement on memorials in [Cemetery / Local Authority] and surrounding parish churchyards.

Persons at risk

  • Operatives of Hallowed Ground Memorial Care.
  • Cemetery visitors and groundskeeping staff.
  • The general public passing through the site.

Risk register

Hazard Risk L × S = R* Control measures Residual
Slips, trips, falls on uneven ground Sprains, fractures from soft / uneven cemetery ground, hidden kerb edges, gravel paths 3 × 2 = 6 Sturdy waterproof boots with grip. Visual inspection of work area on arrival. Kit kept in a single tray rather than scattered. Avoid working in fading daylight. 2
Unstable or leaning memorials Falling stone causing crush / impact injury 2 × 4 = 8 Pre-work stability check by pushing top corner of the memorial gently. If movement is detected, work is stopped, the family and cemetery office are notified in writing, and the job is declined until a NAMM-registered mason has assessed it. 3
Manual handling of equipment Back injury lifting water containers, kit boxes, tool trays 3 × 2 = 6 Water carried in 5L containers rather than larger. Bend at knees, no twisting. Equipment transported on a sack barrow from vehicle to grave where ground allows. 2
Chemical contact — biocide Skin or eye irritation from D2 Biological Solution 2 × 2 = 4 Nitrile gloves and safety glasses worn during application. SDS held on vehicle. No mixing with other products. Eyewash and clean water carried on site. Hands washed after every application. 2
Lone working Delayed help in the event of injury, collapse or assault 2 × 3 = 6 Phone-based check-in with a designated contact every 2 hours. Mobile carried at all times. Cemetery office advised of arrival and expected departure where staffed. 3
Weather — cold, wet, heat Hypothermia, sunstroke, slips on wet stone 2 × 2 = 4 Work scheduled around forecast. No work in heavy rain (biocide ineffective and slip risk rises). Layered clothing in winter. Drinking water and sunhat carried in summer. 2
Members of the public — mourners on site Distress caused by visible work, accidental obstruction of paths 3 × 1 = 3 Work paused if a mourner is at an adjacent grave. Acknowledge politely, offer to step away. Equipment never left on or across paths. 2
Hidden services — lawnmower cables, irrigation Cuts to buried cables / pipes if any digging undertaken 1 × 3 = 3 No digging or ground penetration is part of standard work. Floral arrangements placed in existing vases or on the surface. 2
Biohazards — sharps, needles Cuts / disease exposure from discarded sharps in cemeteries 1 × 3 = 3 Visual sweep of work area before kneeling or placing kit. Heavy-duty gloves carried. Sharps not handled directly; cemetery office notified if found. 1
Vehicle — loading / driving with kit RTA, kit shifting in transit 2 × 3 = 6 Kit secured in load space, water containers strapped. Vehicle serviced annually. Phone use hands-free only. 2

* Likelihood × Severity = Risk. Each rated 1 (low) to 5 (high). Residual risk is the score after control measures are applied.

PPE carried on every visit

  • Nitrile gloves (and heavy-duty gardening gloves for waste / sharps).
  • Safety glasses for chemical application.
  • Waterproof boots with grip.
  • Knee pads.
  • High-visibility waistcoat when working near vehicle access routes.

Emergency procedure

  1. Stop work and make the area safe.
  2. Call 999 for medical emergencies; alert the cemetery office.
  3. Notify designated contact — [Name, phone].
  4. Record details in incident log within 24 hours.
